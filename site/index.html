    How it works

    Twice a day we fetch five years of weekly figures and the last 90 days of daily figures from Google Trends for “All I Want for Christmas Is You” in the country, and put both on the same scale. The Festivity Index is the past week’s average as a share of a typical Christmas-week peak. Snowfall on this page is proportional to it.

    About the data

    Google Trends is a brilliant free source, but it has quirks worth knowing before you read too much into any single day.

    It’s relative, not a count
    Trends scores interest from 0 to 100, where 100 is the busiest moment in whatever period you ask about, and never reveals how many searches that is. Every request gets its own 100, so we line the weekly and daily figures up using the weeks they share, and measure everything against past Christmases.
    It’s a sample
    Google estimates interest from a sample of searches, so asking the same question a few hours apart gives slightly different answers. Each day on the chart is the median of up to a week of readings, but the index can still move a point or so with no real change in mood.
    Quiet days round to zero
    Figures are whole numbers, so on the daily scale a quiet October day can show as 0. That means too few searches to measure, not none, so we use the weekly figure for those days.
    Now and then it’s just wrong
    Occasionally a response has zeros in the middle of a busy week. We check every response against the weekly figures and skip any that don’t add up, keeping the previous data until a good one arrives.
    Recent days are provisional
    Today is only part of a day, and the latest days have fewer readings behind them, so they settle over the following week. Festive lift-off is fixed only once the week after it has passed.
    It measures interest in one song
    Searches also react to news, adverts, a viral video or Mariah herself. The song has been a remarkably steady festive barometer year after year, but it stands in for the national mood rather than measuring it directly.
